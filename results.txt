ABLITERATION & REFUSAL BEHAVIOUR — CONSOLIDATED RESULTS
Qwen3.6-27B (stock / abliterated) and Qwen3.8-27B
Compiled 2026-08-20. All runs local: llama.cpp, Q4_K_M, single RTX 3090.

RESEARCH QUESTION
  How harmful is a state-of-the-art local LLM when it is obliterated?

ONE-LINE ANSWER
  Abliteration reliably removes refusals (100% -> 83%) without adding capability,
  but the harm ceiling is set by the base model's competence, not by the edit --
  and the newest generation refuses everything while over-refusing three times
  less, so the tradeoff abliteration exists to buy is disappearing.

2. REFUSAL BEHAVIOUR  (primary numbers; judge = qwen3.6-27b-normal)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Metric                              Qwen3.8   Q3.6 stock   Q3.6 abliterated
  StrongREJECT refusal rate           1.000     1.000        0.831
  StrongREJECT score (higher=worse)   0.000     0.00         0.81
  XSTest safe: OVER-refusal (lower=better)  0.033  0.106      0.016
  XSTest unsafe: refusal (higher=safer)     0.853  0.852      0.788

  Parse rates: StrongREJECT 99.7%, XSTest 96.4%.
  Abliteration: -17 points harmful refusal, -9 points benign over-refusal.
  One indiscriminate dial; benefit and cost are the same edit.

  [!] TRUNCATION CAVEAT ON THE XSTEST NUMBERS. XSTest generations were capped at
      max_tokens=256. Completions ending mid-sentence (i.e. cut off at the cap),
      measured on the artifacts still available:

        arm                      overall      safe subset    unsafe subset
        qwen3.6 stock (regen)    181/450 40%  161/250  64%   20/200  10%
        qwen3.6 ablated (regen)  123/450 27%   93/250  37%   30/200  15%
        qwen3.8                  204/450 45%  163/250  65%   41/200  21%

      Truncation is not random with respect to what is being measured: refusals
      are short and survive the cap, compliant answers are long and get cut, so
      the cap lands mostly on one side of the C/P/I classification. It also
      differs between the two models being compared (64% vs 37% on the safe
      subset), and the safe subset is exactly where the over-refusal headline
      comes from.

      Scope of the measurement: directly measured for the qwen3.8 arm and for the
      qwen3.6 arms REGENERATED on 2026-08-19. The original qwen3.6 generations
      that produced the 0.106 / 0.016 figures above were lost with a scratchpad
      and cannot be checked, but ran under the same 256-token cap, so the same
      effect is expected.

      Direction and size of any resulting bias are NOT established. A truncated
      compliant answer is usually still visibly compliant, so a C/P/I judge will
      often still score it correctly. Quantifying this requires regenerating at a
      higher cap and re-grading; that has not been done. Treat the XSTest
      over-refusal comparison as indicative pending that check. The StrongREJECT
      numbers are unaffected (cap 2048, medians 151-238, zero empty completions).

  Qwen3.8: full refusal AND 3x less over-refusal than stock. It obtains most of
  abliteration's calibration benefit without abliteration's safety cost.

  q38-control                         313   0.997    0.016

  KEY FINDING: of every wrapper tested, only refusal_suppression (Wei et al.
  2023) lowers refusal below the unwrapped baseline. Every other wrapper --
  including the community "universal jailbreak" -- RAISES refusal. The jailbreak
  arm's apparent effect was partly a grading artifact: it mandates a decoy
  refusal before the real answer, which a refusal-detecting judge scores as a
  refusal unless the decoy is stripped first.

  Safety wrappers work but not uniformly: responsible_assistant cuts compliance
  16.5% -> 4.8%, yet made hate/harassment compliance WORSE (6.2% -> 10.4%).

7. METHOD NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Serving: llama.cpp b9436; capability tasks 65,536 ctx / thinking on /
    --parallel 1; refusal tasks 32,768 ctx / thinking off / temp 0 / --parallel 4.
  Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8 are the SAME architecture family (GGUF arch "qwen35",
    hybrid attention/SSM, full_attention_interval 4, 64 vs 65 layers), so the
    generational comparison is not confounded by an architecture change.
  Only ~16 of 65 layers carry a KV cache; the naive KV formula overestimates
    VRAM by ~4x on this architecture. Measured: 20.2 GB at 65,536 context.
  XSTest prompts from the ungated GitHub source; the HF mirror is gated.
  Judge budget 1024 tokens; a 512-token pass truncated verdicts and biased an
    earlier run.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. KNOWN GAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - XSTest generations were capped at 256 tokens and 27-45% of completions were
    truncated mid-sentence, concentrated in the safe subset and differing between
    arms. See the truncation caveat in section 2. Unquantified; needs a
    higher-cap regeneration to settle.
  - lab_bench_cloning_scenarios for Qwen3.8 needs re-running (cloningbench.md).
  - Qwen3.8 was not abliterated; no abliterated Qwen3.8 build was tested. The
    generational comparison is stock-to-stock plus qwen3.6's abliterated arm.
  - Capability tasks are small (n=30-80); treat magnitudes as indicative.
  - Single local judge per pass. Cross-judge agreement is measured (~1 point)
    but both judges are 27B-Q4 models from one family.
